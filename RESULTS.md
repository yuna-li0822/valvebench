# RESULTS

This is the full accounting: what was measured, what changed between the first and second audit passes, and why. Numbers are pulled directly from generated artifacts (`leaderboard.md`, `experiments/pinnvalve_ablation_report.md`, `experiments/pinnvalve_ablation_results.json`) — nothing here is hand-typed without a corresponding regeneratable source.

## 1. VALVEBENCH leaderboard (dataset v0.2: CDC anthropometrics + Pediatric Heart Network annulus model)

`aipv/heart_valve_project/leaderboard.md`, generated by `valvebench.tasks.run_multi_seed` across 5 seeds (0–4), mean ± SD, `n_train=200`, `n_ood=80` per seed:

| model | ID RMSE | ID MAE | ID R² | OOD RMSE | OOD MAE | OOD R² |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| linear | 2.65 ± 0.17 | 2.12 ± 0.17 | 0.42 ± 0.13 | 3.02 ± 0.27 | 2.33 ± 0.15 | 0.68 ± 0.05 |
| ridge | 2.64 ± 0.17 | 2.12 ± 0.17 | 0.43 ± 0.12 | 2.90 ± 0.16 | 2.26 ± 0.09 | 0.71 ± 0.04 |
| mlp | 2.66 ± 0.15 | 2.12 ± 0.17 | 0.41 ± 0.12 | 2.88 ± 0.11 | 2.23 ± 0.07 | 0.71 ± 0.04 |
| gbm | 2.98 ± 0.28 | 2.36 ± 0.25 | 0.26 ± 0.20 | 3.04 ± 0.31 | 2.42 ± 0.24 | 0.68 ± 0.04 |
| gbm_monotone | 2.76 ± 0.21 | 2.18 ± 0.21 | 0.36 ± 0.18 | 2.96 ± 0.31 | 2.35 ± 0.21 | 0.70 ± 0.04 |
| pinn | 3.02 ± 0.32 | 2.38 ± 0.26 | 0.26 ± 0.08 | 3.23 ± 0.30 | 2.57 ± 0.20 | 0.64 ± 0.07 |

**Reading this honestly:**
- The patient data are now sampled from real reference distributions (CDC growth charts for height/weight; the Pediatric Heart Network pulmonary-annulus model for valve size — see §2b and `CITATIONS.md`). Children of the same size differ in annulus diameter by about ±2.4 mm (SD 0.24 cm × √BSA), and that spread cannot be predicted from height and weight. An estimator that knows each child's true BSA but not their annulus z-score has an irreducible RMSE of ≈ 2.4–2.5 mm on this data. The best models (`linear`/`ridge`/`mlp`, ID RMSE 2.64–2.66 mm) are within ≈ 0.2 mm of that floor, and ID R² ≈ 0.42 is about what that spread allows. Low R² here reflects real biological variation, not a modeling failure.
- `linear`/`ridge`/`mlp` are statistically indistinguishable on both ID and OOD.
- `gbm`, `gbm_monotone` and `pinn` are worse on ID (RMSE 2.76–3.02). On OOD everything except `pinn` is within about one SD of the linear family. `pinn` (the monotonic-growth-prior MLP for this patient task) is the weakest on OOD (RMSE 3.23 ± 0.30). Under the earlier v0.1 data it ranked between linear and GBM; that ranking did not persist, so it should not be cited as an advantage of the physics prior on this task.
- OOD R² is higher than ID R² for most models. That is an R²-with-different-test-sets artifact (the OOD set spans a wider age range and so has more target variance), not evidence of better generalization; RMSE is the fairer cross-set comparison, and OOD RMSE is worse than ID RMSE for every model.

**Physiology check** (`tests/test_annular_growth_task.py`, controlled feature-holding-fixed grid, n=200, seed=42): `gbm_monotone` shows **zero** monotonicity violations vs. unconstrained `gbm`'s **~19%** — confirms `monotonic_cst` does what it claims.

### Previous leaderboard (v0.1 generator, kept for the record)

Under the earlier hand-written generator (`anthropometrics="linear"`, `annulus_source="legacy"`, still reproducible and covered by a checksum test) the same six models scored ID RMSE 0.82–0.89 mm, ID R² 0.66–0.71, OOD RMSE 0.90–1.38 mm. Those errors were small because that generator made annulus size an almost deterministic function of BSA, and it grew the annulus by about 5.5 mm per year. See §2b.

## 2. Two generator bugs found and fixed (this is what R² going from ~0.01 to ~0.70 means)

Before this audit pass, the leaderboard's R² was **≈0.01 for linear/ridge, and negative for mlp/gbm/pinn** — every model was at or below the "predict the mean" baseline. Root cause, confirmed empirically:

```
correlation(Age_yrs, Valve_t0)  = 0.07   (should carry signal; was noise-level)
correlation(BSA_m2,  Valve_t0)  = 0.02   (should carry signal; was noise-level)
correlation(Age_yrs, Height_cm) = 0.06   (should be ~1.0 for a real pediatric population)
```

`Valve_t0` was drawn as `Uniform(15, 30)`, independent of every feature — since `Valve_t12 = Valve_t0 + growth_rate·12 + noise`, that independent baseline dominated `Valve_t12`'s variance too, swamping the growth-rate signal that *was* feature-dependent. Separately, `Height_cm`/`Weight_kg` were drawn independently of `Age_yrs`, so a synthetic "1-year-old" could be assigned a height of 180cm.

**Fix**: `Valve_t0 = 6.0 + 14.0·sqrt(BSA) + noise` (the qualitative basis of real pediatric annulus Z-score methodology), and height/weight now scale linearly with age (reusing the same age-correlated model already present elsewhere in this project, `legacy/utils/synthetic_dataset_generator.py`, rather than inventing a new one). After the fix:

```
correlation(Age_yrs, Height_cm) = 0.97
correlation(Age_yrs, Weight_kg) = 0.96
correlation(BSA_m2,  Valve_t0)  = 0.93
holdout R² (plain linear regression, 400/500 split) = 0.85
```

## 2b. Third pass: literature-derived patient generator (dataset v0.2)

The remaining weakness in the patient side was that its formulas were illustrative (`Height = 75 + 6·age`, `Valve_t0 = 6 + 14·√BSA`, a hand-written growth law). v0.2 replaces them with sourced pieces:

- **Height and weight** are sampled from the real CDC growth-chart LMS tables (birth–36 months and 2–20 years; data bundled in `data/cdc/` with SHA256s). The median 10-year-old is ≈ 138 cm and 32 kg, matching CDC; the old linear guess overstated weight in young children (e.g. ≈ 25 kg at age 6 vs ≈ 20 kg).
- **Annulus diameter** follows the Pediatric Heart Network normal-echo model, Lopez et al. 2017, Table 2 (pulmonary annulus short axis): D = (1.91 + z·0.24) cm × BSA^0.50, constants confirmed against the paper.
- **12-month growth** is no longer a formula. Each child keeps the same CDC percentiles and the same annulus z-score for 12 months (an assumption), so the annulus grows as their body grows.

**What this exposed.** The old growth law added ≈ 5.5 mm per year (mean 12-month annulus ≈ 25.5 mm). The literature-based version gives ≈ 0.75 mm per year (mean ≈ 19.7 mm), as expected from a model where annulus size scales with √BSA (a 10-year-old adding about 0.1 m² of BSA in a year gains about 1 mm). Diameters and growth in the v0.1 figures and tables were therefore too large; they are superseded by v0.2. The scaffold side (PINNVALVE) does not use this generator, so its results are unchanged.

**Assumptions that are not from any source** (also in `CITATIONS.md`): height–weight z-score correlation 0.6; percentile tracking over 12 months; annulus z-score independent of height/weight z-scores; 0.3 mm measurement noise; uniform age and 50/50 sex; sex and age effects beyond BSA ignored. The PHN model describes **healthy** children, not congenital heart disease patients, whose annulus is often smaller or abnormal.

## 3. A baseline bug found and fixed: `MLPRegressor` had no feature scaling

Once the generator fix gave the benchmark real signal, `mlp` scored **R² ≈ −7.3** on the leaderboard — catastrophically worse than every other model, including its own physics-informed variant (`pinn`, R² 0.69) built on the same underlying network type. Root cause: `sklearn.neural_network.MLPRegressor` was fed raw features spanning wildly different scales (`Height_cm` ~45–190, `Weight_kg` ~2–60, `BMI` ~10–25, `BSA_m2` ~0.2–2.0) with no standardization — a textbook cause of gradient-based-optimizer failure, not evidence MLPs are a bad fit for this task.

**Fix**: standardize X and y (fit on train only, per `fit()` call) before/after `MLPRegressor`, and raise `max_iter` from 500 to 2000 (500 was insufficient once there was real signal to converge on). `RidgeRegression` got the same standardization fix for a related but distinct reason: L2 penalizes raw coefficients, so an unscaled feature's effective regularization strength depended on its arbitrary units.

**Result**: `mlp` now performs on par with `linear`/`ridge` (R² 0.71 ID, matching exactly) — confirming this was purely a preprocessing bug. See `tests/test_leakage.py::test_baseline_mlp_normalization_uses_train_statistics_only` for the regression test verifying the fix doesn't leak test statistics into normalization.

## 4. PINNVALVE ablation — final, corrected numbers

`experiments/pinnvalve_ablation_report.md`, 5 seeds (0–4), `physics_weight=1.0`, held-out ID test (24 designs, never trained on) + untouched OOD test (16 corners of the full design space):

| model | ID RMSE (mm) | OOD RMSE (mm) | OOD ξ RMSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| mlp_only (physics_weight=0) | 0.212 ± 0.035 | 1.944 ± 0.098 | 0.519 ± 0.053 |
| PINNVALVE | 0.223 ± 0.021 | 1.962 ± 0.067 | 0.206 ± 0.011 |

- **OOD diameter RMSE improvement: −1.1% ± 4.2%** (1/5 seeds positive). Not a reliable effect.
- **OOD martensite-fraction (ξ) RMSE improvement: 60.3%**, and this held at *every* `physics_weight` from 0.1 to 20 tested during development, and at every seed. ξ is never directly supervised — the network only ever sees diameter as a training target.

## 5. What changed between the first and second audit passes on PINNVALVE (read this before citing the 13% number anywhere — it's wrong)

An earlier version of this project reported "~13% OOD RMSE improvement, robust across a 5-seed sweep." Two real methodology bugs were found behind that number on a second, skeptical pass:

1. **Hyperparameter tuning leaked into the test set.** `physics_weight` (and `hidden_dim`, indirectly) had been selected by directly observing OOD RMSE on `ood_corner_dataset_sma(seed=123)` — the exact dataset then used to report final results. Any hyperparameter chosen this way is optimized to look good on that specific dataset, which is not the same as generalizing.
2. **"ID RMSE" was training error.** `run_ablation`'s `id_true`/`id_pred` were computed by predicting on `train_df` itself — literally the data the model was fit on — not a held-out split.

**Fix**: `valvebench.datasets.synthetic.interior_train_val_test_split_sma` splits interior-box designs by `sample_id` into train/val/test (grouped, so all 5 timepoints of a design stay together); `ood_shell_dataset` provides a validation OOD set (random samples outside the training box) strictly disjoint from the final `ood_corner_dataset_sma` test set. `physics_weight` was re-swept using *only* train/val/shell:

| physics_weight | OOD diameter improvement (validation shell, 7 seeds) |
| ---: | --- |
| 0.2 | mean −1.7%, 3/7 positive |
| 0.3 | mean −0.8%, 4/7 positive |
| 1.0 | (selected: modest, positive median, preserves full ξ benefit) |
| 3.0 | mean +0.9% ± 7.5% (1 run) |
| 10.0 (previous default) | mean −17.2% ± 11.1% — actively harmful once evaluated honestly |

The diameter effect **did not survive** at any weight tested. The ξ-recovery effect (mlp_only ξ RMSE ≈ 0.55 vs. physics-on ≈ 0.11–0.19) **did survive**, unchanged, at every weight. `TrainConfig`'s default was changed from `physics_weight=10.0` to `physics_weight=1.0` as a result — see that dataclass's docstring in `valvebench/models/pinn_scaffold.py` for the full sweep.

**The honest conclusion**: this project's original PINN-beats-MLP-on-OOD-diameter headline does not hold up. What does hold up, robustly, is that physics residuals let a network recover latent physical state it was never shown — a real, defensible, if less flashy, PINN result. See `experiments/figures/physics_weight_sweep.png`.

## 6. Where the physics prior *does* reliably help: data scarcity and noise

Two follow-up experiments, run after the corrected ablation, asking a more specific question than "does physics help on average" — "does it help more in a specific regime?"

**Data efficiency** (`experiments/figures/data_efficiency.png`, 3 seeds per point, fixed held-out ID/OOD test sets, varying only the number of training scaffold designs):

| training designs | mlp_only OOD RMSE | PINNVALVE OOD RMSE | improvement |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 6 | 1.92 | 1.71 | +24.1% |
| 12 | 1.32 | 1.37 | −4.2% |
| 24 | 1.37 | 1.33 | +2.4% |
| 48 | 1.27 | 1.21 | +4.5% |
| 96 | 1.17 | 1.15 | +1.5% |

**Noise robustness** (fixed val/test/shell sets at baseline noise; varying only training-data observation noise, 3 seeds per point):

| noise_scale | mlp_only OOD RMSE | PINNVALVE OOD RMSE | improvement |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.05 | 1.226 | 1.163 | +5.2% |
| 0.10 | 1.240 | 1.164 | +6.1% |
| 0.30 | 1.324 | 1.167 | +11.8% |
| 0.60 | 1.602 | 1.229 | +23.3% |
| 1.00 | 2.312 | 1.259 | +45.5% |

**This is the more coherent and more interesting finding than the flat "OOD improvement" claim**: the physics prior acts as a regularizer that resists being pulled around by noisy or scarce data. As training data gets noisier, the physics-constrained model degrades far more slowly than the data-only model (whose error more than doubles over the noise range tested, vs. PINNVALVE's much flatter curve). The data-efficiency trend is directionally consistent (large benefit at 6 designs, fading toward zero by ~24+) but noisier than the noise-robustness trend, and both are based on only 3 seeds per condition — read individual percentages as suggestive, not tightly bounded, but the monotonic pattern across 5 well-separated noise levels is a much stronger signal than a coin flip.

**Caveat stated plainly**: this is a controlled synthetic benchmark where the physics residual's functional form matches the generator used to label the data by construction (see `valvebench/physics/sma_constitutive.py`'s docstring). It demonstrates that a *correct* physics prior helps most exactly where classical statistics says a prior should help most (small n, high noise) — a coherent and expected result, not a claim of calibration against real nitinol or tissue behavior.

## 7b. Error analysis: where specifically does each model fail?

`experiments/error_analysis.py` breaks per-corner OOD error down by design-parameter region (single seed, `experiments/error_analysis_report.md`). Two findings:

1. **Both models fail hardest on the exact same three designs** — the corners with `Suture_Count=4, Material_Stiffness=0.5` (least mechanical constraint at crimping, the most extreme low-constraint corner in the design space). This is the design-space region furthest from "typical" behavior, and neither model handles it well.
2. **PINNVALVE is not more accurate in any specific parameter region** at this seed — mlp_only has lower MAE in 7 of 8 low/high region splits (Suture_Count, Material_Stiffness, Dissolution_Rate both sides, Fold_Angle low side); PINNVALVE is only better on the Fold_Angle-high split (+4.7%). This is consistent with, and a finer-grained confirmation of, §5's finding that the diameter-accuracy improvement doesn't hold up — it's not that PINNVALVE wins in some regions and loses in others that average out to zero; it's that mlp_only is slightly ahead almost everywhere at this seed.

This reinforces §5's honest conclusion rather than complicating it: the physics prior's value in this project is latent-state recovery and robustness under scarcity/noise (§6), not region-specific or aggregate diameter accuracy.
