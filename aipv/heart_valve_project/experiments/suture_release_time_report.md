\* The exponential fit never reaches zero, so for this low load it outlasts complete absorption; the suture cannot hold past absorption, so its release is set to the absorption window.

**Single-strand check for the double-stranded design:** if one strand carried the full 14.3 N, load/strength = 0.54 and release would come at 8 wk (1.8 mo) (linear-to-zero) or 7 wk (1.6 mo) (exponential).

**Uncertainty, in both directions.** The USP value is a minimum; real sutures are usually stronger, which delays release. But in series the row lets go at the *first* break, so strength scatter between sutures, knot weakening, and in-vivo loading (cyclic, higher than the static crimp load) all bring release earlier. Retention after 6 weeks is extrapolated.

**Compared with growth:** expanding from 13 to 26 mm takes about 15 years of annular growth (Discussion §4.1). Every design releases within the first year.
