## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Overview

The project has three parts:

1. **VALVEBENCH, a patient-growth benchmark.** Models predict a child's pulmonary annulus diameter now and
   12 months later from body size.
2. **PINNVALVE, a physics-informed neural network for the scaffold.** It predicts the diameter of a
   growth-adaptive nitinol valve scaffold over the 12 months after implant, as its constraining sutures
   dissolve.
3. **Finite-element simulations in COMSOL Multiphysics 6.4.** These test the assumptions behind the
   scaffold model, evaluate the frame geometry, and provide independent training and test data for
   PINNVALVE.

All models, splits and simulations are seeded and reproducible from the repository.

### 2.2 Patient-growth data and task (VALVEBENCH)

**Data.** Synthetic children aged 1–16 years were sampled from published reference distributions:

- **Body size.** Height and weight come from the CDC growth-chart LMS tables (birth–36 months and
  2–20 years). The height and weight z-scores are correlated at 0.6.
- **Annulus diameter.** It follows the Pediatric Heart Network normal-echocardiogram model for the
  pulmonary annulus (Lopez et al., 2017): D = (1.91 + 0.24·z) cm × BSA^0.50.
- **Growth.** Each child keeps their growth-chart percentiles and annulus z-score for 12 months, so the
  annulus grows with the body.
- **Measurement noise.** 0.3 mm is added.

**Task.** The inputs are height, weight, BMI and body-surface area (BSA). The outputs are the annulus
diameter at 0 and 12 months.

**Splits.** In-distribution (ID) children are aged 3–13 years and split by patient. The
out-of-distribution (OOD) set contains the excluded age extremes (1–3 and 13–16 years). Each seed uses
200 in-distribution children (split by patient into train and test) and 80 OOD children.

**Models.**
- linear regression
- ridge regression (standardised inputs)
- a multilayer perceptron (MLP; standardised inputs and outputs)
- histogram gradient boosting, unconstrained and with monotonicity constraints
- a neural network with a monotonic-growth penalty ("pinn")

### 2.3 Reduced-order scaffold model (synthetic data)

**Device.** The scaffold is crimped and held by absorbable sutures. As they dissolve, the constraint stress
falls as σ(t) = σ₀·exp(−k·t), with σ₀ = 150 MPa × (N/7) × material stiffness.

**Material response.** The martensite fraction ξ follows Brinson's cosine reverse-transformation
kinetics. Recoverable strain follows the Auricchio–Sacco closure σ = E(ξ)(ε − ε_L·ξ), with:

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| E_A / E_M | 60 / 25 GPa |
| ε_L (maximum transformation strain) | 6% |
| Reverse transformation window | 100–40 MPa |

A literature preset was also run (Bernini et al., 2023).

**From material to device.** The recovered-strain fraction is mapped onto a design-dependent diameter
range (D₀ to D_max).

**Design variables.**
- suture count N: 4–11
- material stiffness: 0.5–2.5
- dissolution rate k: 0.05–0.3 per month
- fold angle: 15–60°

**Dataset.** 120 designs from an interior sub-box were split by design into 72 train, 24 validation and
24 test designs. The 16 corners of the full box form the OOD test set. Each design was observed at months
0, 3, 6, 9 and 12, with 0.1 mm noise.

### 2.4 PINNVALVE

**Network.** A fully connected network (2 hidden layers, tanh) maps the design vector and time to three
outputs: diameter D, constraint stress σ and martensite fraction ξ. **Only D is supervised.**

**Loss.** The loss is the diameter mean squared error plus a weighted physics term. The physics weight
ramps up linearly over the first epochs. The physics term contains:
- the Brinson kinetics residual
- the constitutive residual
- penalties for a shrinking diameter and for exceeding the design's saturation diameter

**Ablation.** Setting the physics weight to 0 gives a data-only network with the identical architecture.

**Hyperparameters.** The physics weight was chosen as 1.0 using only a validation OOD set (random designs
outside the interior box, disjoint from the corner test set). Architecture, learning rate and epoch count
were set by hand and not tuned on any test set.

**Evaluation.** Diameter error is reported on held-out ID designs and OOD corners. The ξ output is scored
against the generator's true value, which the network never sees.

### 2.5 Finite-element models (COMSOL)

Models were written with the COMSOL Java API and solved by COMSOL's batch solver. This is the same solver
as the desktop application, and every model file opens there. All models use COMSOL's Lagoudas
shape-memory-alloy material at 37 °C:

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| E_A / E_M | 47 / 20 GPa |
| Ms / Mf / As / Af | −10 / −25 / 10 / 25 °C |
| C_M / C_A | 6 / 7 MPa/K |
| Maximum transformation strain | 6% |
| σ* | 200 MPa |

Geometric nonlinearity was included. The transformation direction was set to forward during crimping and
reverse afterwards, because COMSOL's automatic setting failed to converge on unloading.

**Ring model.** A solid nitinol ring (inner radius 11 mm, outer radius 13 mm, height 5 mm) was modelled
in 2D-axisymmetry, which is exact for this geometry and uniform radial loading. It used 8 × 20
quadratic elements. Three studies run in sequence, each starting from the previous state:

1. **Crimp:** the outer radius is pushed inward by U₀.
2. **Hand-over:** a stiff spring anchored at the crimped radius is relaxed in about 110 steps, so the sutures
   take over.
3. **Release:** from month 0 to 12, the suture stiffness decays.

The sutures are a radial spring on the outer surface:
- stiffness per area: K(t) = K₀·exp(−k·t)
- K₀ = s·N·E_s·A_s/(L_s·A_out), with E_s = 1 GPa, suture diameter 0.3 mm, L_s = 5 mm
- s = 1 represents realistic sutures

Outputs at each step: mean outer diameter, volume-averaged and maximum ξ, peak von Mises stress, and
suture pressure.

**Lattice-cell model.** The rendered frame is a closed diamond lattice:
- 12 cells around a 26 mm frame
- struts about 5.6 mm long and about 0.4 mm wide (estimated from the render)
- X-shaped junctions

One quarter of a cell was modelled, unrolled flat in plane stress, 0.4 mm thick. It contains one strut
between a quarter of the central junction and a quarter of the corner junction, with rounded (filleted)
corners. Mirror symmetry on all four cell edges makes this exact for a uniformly crimped lattice; the top
edge moves axially as a rigid line, so the cell can lengthen freely.

The hoop direction was crimped from 26 to 13 mm (the planned implant diameter), then released.

**Outputs:**
- peak in-plane principal strain, compared with nitinol's 6–8% recoverable range
- ξ and von Mises stress
- hoop force, from the integrated first Piola–Kirchhoff stress

**Variations.** Strut width, fillet radius, mesh density, cell count, strut length, and a linear strut
taper (wide at the junction, narrow at mid-length).

**Reporting rule.** Recovery is reported only for designs that stay under 6%, because the material model
has no plasticity.

**Verification.**
- mesh refinement (three meshes per model)
- load-step halving
- full recovery with negligible sutures
- a no-dissolution control (k = 0)
- comparison of the ring model against an independently hand-built 3D COMSOL model of the same ring

### 2.6 COMSOL training dataset and PINNVALVE on finite-element data

**Dataset.** 136 ring designs were simulated with s = 1000. That is the regime where the sutures hold
part of the crimp, so the ring stays partly martensitic at implant (Section 3.3). The varied quantities
were:

| Variable | Range |
|---|---|
| Suture count N | 4–11 |
| Dissolution rate k | 0.05–0.3 per month |
| Crimp depth U₀ | 0.3–0.7 mm |
| Austenite finish temperature Af | 20–30 °C |

Suture stiffness was held fixed because, in this model, it is not separable from suture count. 120 designs
were Latin-hypercube samples of an interior sub-box (N 5–9, k 0.08–0.25, U₀ 0.38–0.62 mm,
Af 22–28 °C), and the 16 full-box corners formed the OOD set. Each design was recorded at 25 times
(0–12 months).

**Splits.** By design: 72 train, 24 validation, 24 test.

**Physics prior.** It is a reduced-order model of the ring, written independently of the simulator.
Lengths are in mm, stresses in MPa, and σ is the compressive mean hoop stress:

1. **Hoop equilibrium:** σ = p·R_out/t_wall. This is exact for the mean hoop stress of a ring under
   external pressure.
2. **Suture law:** p = K₀(N)·exp(−k·t)·(U₀ − (R_out − D/2)).
3. **One-dimensional constitutive closure:** σ = E(ξ)(ε − H·ξ), with ε = (R_out − D/2)/R_mean.
4. **One-sided constraints:**
   - D does not decrease and ξ does not increase during release
   - ξ is at most the Brinson reverse-kinetics bound at σ, with σ_Af = C_A(37 − Af)
   - D does not exceed its free value

The suture law is shared with the simulator. **The parameters are also the simulator's:** ring geometry,
suture stiffness scale (K₀) and material constants (E_A, E_M, H, C_A, A_s) are copied from the COMSOL model,
not fitted or chosen independently. The finite-element solution and the full axisymmetric Lagoudas response
are not shared; the physics loss replaces them with the 1D closure above.

**Network and training.** Same architecture as Section 2.4, with 32 hidden units. Inputs are N, k, U₀, Af
and t; only D is supervised. Training ran for 2000 epochs at a learning rate of 5×10⁻³, with physics
weight 1.0 ramped over the first 400 epochs. All of these were fixed before any test set was evaluated.

**Experiments.**
- **Main ablation:** physics weight 1.0 vs 0, 5 seeds.
- **Data scarcity:** 4, 8, 16 or 32 training designs, 3 seeds each.
- **Sparse labels:** D supervised only at months 0, 6 and 12, with physics enforced at all 25 times;
  3 seeds.
- **Noise:** 0.03 mm Gaussian noise on training diameters, 3 seeds.
- **Benchmark baselines:** the generic VALVEBENCH models were also fit on the same inputs.

ξ is scored against COMSOL's volume-averaged martensite fraction, which no model is trained on.

**Post-hoc ξ baseline.** The data-only network's ξ output receives no gradient, so it is not a fair
comparison for PINNVALVE's ξ. The baseline we report instead takes the data-only network's predicted D and
computes ξ from it after training, with the same equations as the physics loss (suture law and closure
solved for ξ; the one-sided kinetics bound is not applied, since clipping to it made the baseline worse). The same baseline is reported on synthetic data, where ξ is
solved from the D → ε mapping, the closure and the Brinson kinetics together.

### 2.7 Evaluation

- **Metrics:** root-mean-square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) in mm, and R² for the
  patient task.
- **Reporting:** results are the mean ± standard deviation over seeds.
- **Physics benefit:** reported as the per-seed percentage change in OOD diameter RMSE, with the
  number of seeds in which it was positive.
- **Leakage prevention:** all splits are by patient or by design, so no individual contributes to two
  splits. Normalisation statistics are fit on training data only. Dedicated tests guard against each of
  these.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Patient-growth benchmark

**Table 1.** VALVEBENCH patient-growth task, annulus diameter (mm), 5 seeds.

| Model | ID RMSE | ID R² | OOD RMSE | OOD R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | 2.65 ± 0.17 | 0.42 ± 0.13 | 3.02 ± 0.27 | 0.68 ± 0.05 |
| Ridge | 2.64 ± 0.17 | 0.43 ± 0.12 | 2.90 ± 0.16 | 0.71 ± 0.04 |
| MLP | 2.66 ± 0.15 | 0.41 ± 0.12 | 2.88 ± 0.11 | 0.71 ± 0.04 |
| Gradient boosting | 2.98 ± 0.28 | 0.26 ± 0.20 | 3.04 ± 0.31 | 0.68 ± 0.04 |
| Gradient boosting, monotone | 2.76 ± 0.21 | 0.36 ± 0.18 | 2.96 ± 0.31 | 0.70 ± 0.04 |
| Monotone-penalty network | 3.02 ± 0.32 | 0.26 ± 0.08 | 3.23 ± 0.30 | 0.64 ± 0.07 |

**The best models are close to the noise floor.** Children of the same size differ in annulus diameter
by about ±2.4 mm, which cannot be predicted from body size. That makes the lowest achievable RMSE about
2.4–2.5 mm. The linear, ridge and MLP models (2.64–2.66 mm) are within 0.2 mm of it and statistically
indistinguishable.

**Monotonicity constraints work.** They removed all physiologically implausible predictions (0% vs 19%
for unconstrained gradient boosting).

**OOD R² should not be compared with ID R².** OOD R² exceeds ID R² only because the OOD set spans a
wider age range. OOD RMSE is worse than ID RMSE for every model.

### 3.2 PINNVALVE on synthetic scaffold data

**Table 2.** Synthetic scaffold task, 5 seeds.

| Model | ID diameter RMSE (mm) | OOD diameter RMSE (mm) | OOD ξ RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data-only network | 0.212 ± 0.035 | 1.944 ± 0.098 | 0.519 ± 0.053 |
| PINNVALVE | 0.223 ± 0.021 | 1.962 ± 0.067 | 0.206 ± 0.011 |
| Data-only D + closure ξ (post hoc) | — | — | 0.171 ± 0.038 |

**Diameter: no reliable gain.** Adding physics did not reliably improve OOD diameter error
(−1.1% ± 4.2%, 1 of 5 seeds positive).

**Hidden state: no gain over a fair baseline.** Against the data-only network's own ξ output, physics
looked 60% better. That output is never trained, however. Passing the data-only network's diameter through
the physics equations after training gave 0.171 ± 0.038, slightly better than PINNVALVE (0.206). Across
conditions, PINNVALVE's ξ was better only with heavy training noise (1.0 mm: 0.200 vs 0.285, 3 of 3 seeds),
where its diameter was also better (1.94 vs 2.78 mm); with sparse labels the post-hoc baseline was better
(`experiments/xi_posthoc_synthetic_report.md`). On synthetic data the physics equations invert the
generator exactly, so ξ follows from diameter and carries no independent information.

**An earlier result was a methodology error.** An earlier analysis reported a 13% diameter improvement.
It came from tuning the physics weight on the test set and from reporting training error as test error,
and it disappeared once both were corrected.

**The physics prior helped most under noise and data scarcity** (3 seeds per condition):
- **Noise:** the OOD diameter improvement grew steadily with measurement noise, from +5.2% at 0.05 mm
  to +45.5% at 1.0 mm.
- **Scarcity:** it was +24.1% with 6 training designs and within ±5% from 12 designs upward.
- **Sparse labels:** with diameter labelled only at months 0 and 12, held-out RMSE fell from 1.31 to
  0.82 mm.
- **Far extrapolation:** with each design range widened by 25%, PINNVALVE's ξ error was 43% higher than
  the post-hoc baseline (0.289 vs 0.202; 0 of 5 seeds better). Against the untrained ξ output it had
  appeared 44% lower.

**Material constants:** with the literature nitinol preset, both conclusions were unchanged: no reliable
diameter gain (OOD MAE change −1.4% ± 7.3%) and no ξ gain over the post-hoc baseline (0.235 vs 0.206).

### 3.3 Finite-element results: can sutures hold the scaffold?

**Verification of the ring model.**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Mesh (4 × 10, 8 × 20, 16 × 40 elements) | Diameters agree within 0.00002 mm; ξ within 0.0002 |
| Load-step halving | Results unchanged to five decimal places |
| Negligible sutures | Ring returns to 25.9996 mm (free size 26 mm), ξ to 0 |
| Hand-built 3D model of the same ring | Month-0 suture force 49.6 N vs 49.5 N |

**Realistic sutures cannot hold a solid ring** (Table 3, Figure 1). At realistic stiffness (s = 1) the
sutures supply 0.12 MPa of pressure, against the roughly 20 MPa needed to hold the crimp. The ring
re-expanded to 99.96% of its free size at implant. The ring was held partly crimped only from about
300× realistic stiffness, the point where suture pressure reaches the stress at which nitinol reverts to
austenite.

**Table 3.** Ring diameter (mm) vs suture stiffness (7 sutures, k = 0.15/month, crimped to 25.00 mm).

| Suture stiffness (× realistic) | Suture pressure at implant (MPa) | D at implant | D at 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.12 | 26.00 | 26.00 |
| 100 | 11.7 | 25.96 | 25.99 |
| 300 | 19.0 | 25.52 | 25.98 |
| 1,000 | 22.2 | 25.18 | 25.80 |
| 3,000 | 34.4 | 25.09 | 25.34 |
| 10,000 | 44.3 | 25.04 | 25.14 |

**At 1000× stiffness the ring behaves as a controlled superelastic scaffold.** Its martensite fraction is
0.56 at implant, and its diameter rises steadily as the sutures dissolve.

**Table 4.** Design effects at 1000× stiffness.

| Design | D at implant (mm) | D at 12 months, k = 0.05 | D at 12 months, k = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 sutures | 25.30 | 25.50 | 25.99 |
| 6 sutures | 25.21 | 25.36 | 25.99 |
| 8 sutures | 25.16 | 25.28 | 25.99 |
| 10 sutures | 25.15 | 25.23 | 25.99 |

- **More sutures** held the ring tighter.
- **Faster dissolution** opened it sooner.
- **Without dissolution (k = 0)** the diameter stayed at 25.18321 mm for all 12 months, so all expansion
  comes from dissolution.
- **Af had the largest material effect.** The 12-month diameter was 25.94, 25.80 and 25.66 mm at Af = 20,
  25 and 30 °C.
- **Austenite modulus and maximum transformation strain mattered little** (changes ≤ 0.02 mm).

These directions agree with the synthetic generator. Two of its assumptions do not hold for this device:
- **Full crimp at implant:** realistic sutures cannot hold the ring crimped (above).
- **Recoverable 2× expansion:** the rendered lattice overstrains well before halving its diameter
  (Section 3.4).

### 3.4 Finite-element results: strut strain in the lattice

**The rendered geometry cannot be crimped to 13 mm without permanent set** (Table 5, Figure 2). With
12 cells and 5.6 mm × 0.4 mm struts:
- **Peak strain at 13 mm is 12.6%,** about twice nitinol's recoverable limit. Strain passes 6% at 23.1 mm
  and 8% at 22.5 mm.
- **The result is mesh-converged:** 12.6%, 12.6% and 12.9% at 4, 8 and 16 elements across the strut.
- **It barely depends on fillet radius:** 12.7% at 0.3 mm.

**Where the peak sits.** At full crimp, strain is 12.6% where the strut meets the junction (0.5–1 mm from
the junction centre) and 0.5% at mid-strut. Once the most-bent region transforms it deforms at nearly
constant stress, so bending concentrates there. This is why narrowing the strut lowers the peak far less
than simple beam theory predicts: 9.7% at 0.2 mm width.

**Table 5.** Peak strut strain during a 26 → 13 mm crimp.

| Geometry | Peak strain at 13 mm | Diameter where strain passes 8% (mm) | Load per suture at 13 mm (N) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 cells, 5.6 mm × 0.4 mm (rendered) | 12.6% | 22.5 | 32.6 |
| 12 cells, 5.6 mm × 0.2 mm | 9.7% | 18.7 | 11.1 |
| 24 cells, 5.6 mm × 0.2 mm | 8.4% | 13.7 | 1.3 |
| 12 cells, 8 mm × 0.25 mm | 8.4% | 14.0 | 3.2 |
| 12 cells, 5.6 mm, tapered 0.4 → 0.15 mm | 6.5% | never | 17.7 |
| 12 cells, 10 mm × 0.25 mm | 5.0–5.2% | never | 1.17 |

The sutures are tied one after another around the middle row, so each carries the row's full hoop
tension. For the two proposed designs, this is 1.17 N (10 mm struts) and 17.7 N (taper).

**Longer struts fix the overstrain; tapering nearly does:**

- **12 cells with 10 mm struts** is the only design that stays under 6%:
  - peak strain 5.2% on the standard mesh, 5.0% on a twice-finer mesh, 5.05% with a larger fillet
  - releases back to 26.00 mm with no residual martensite
  - cells about 19 mm tall, and only 1.17 N of hoop tension per row (about 9 kPa equivalent pressure,
    against 0.48 MPa for the rendered geometry)
- **Tapering the original layout's struts** came closest at 0.4 → 0.15 mm (6.5%, inside the 6–8% band)
  and keeps 17.7 N of hoop tension. Wider roots (0.5 → 0.15 mm) and a thinner middle (0.4 → 0.12 mm) were
  worse (8.4% and 7.1%), so no taper got under 6%.

The two designs mark the ends of a trade-off. The outward force that anchors the valve is the same force
the sutures must resist to hold it crimped.

### 3.5 PINNVALVE on finite-element data

All 136 COMSOL designs converged. At implant, diameters ranged from 24.76 to 25.71 mm and martensite from
0.15 to 0.73. At 12 months, diameters ranged from 24.83 to 26.00 mm.

**Validating the reduced physics.** Given COMSOL's own diameter, the reduced-order ring physics reproduced
two quantities it is never fitted to:
- suture pressure: 22.17 vs 22.20 MPa (reference design)
- volume-averaged martensite, within about 0.01 at every month: 0.496 vs 0.492 at implant, 0.097 vs
  0.100 at month 12

**Table 6.** COMSOL scaffold task, 5 seeds (`comsol_leaderboard.md`).

| Model | ID RMSE (mm) | OOD RMSE (mm) | ID ξ RMSE | OOD ξ RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | 0.082 | 0.222 | — | — |
| Ridge | 0.082 | 0.221 | — | — |
| Gradient boosting | 0.064 | 0.221 | — | — |
| MLP (scikit-learn) | 0.050 ± 0.002 | 0.156 ± 0.004 | — | — |
| Data-only network | 0.0088 ± 0.0023 | 0.095 ± 0.016 | 0.256 ± 0.025 | 0.322 ± 0.027 |
| PINNVALVE | 0.0090 ± 0.0011 | 0.086 ± 0.011 | 0.008 ± 0.001 | 0.047 ± 0.004 |
| Data-only D + closure ξ (post hoc) | — | — | 0.006 ± 0.002 | 0.076 ± 0.009 |

The pattern from synthetic data replicates on independent finite-element data:

- **Diameter: no reliable gain.** The OOD change was +7.9% ± 16.0% (3 of 5 seeds positive).
- **Hidden state: a consistent gain over a fair baseline.** PINNVALVE recovered COMSOL's martensite
  fraction to 0.047 RMSE on unseen designs (Figure 3). The fair comparison is the data-only network's
  diameter passed through the same closure after training (0.076), not its untrained ξ output (0.32), so
  the gain is 38%, not 85%. PINNVALVE was better in every seed of every condition with at least 8 training
  designs, but not with 4 (1 of 3 seeds). On held-out interior designs the baseline was as good (0.006 vs
  0.008), so the gain is in extrapolation. It is not explained by diameter accuracy, which differed by
  under 10%. Unlike the synthetic result, this one survives the fair baseline.
- **Noise: the clearest diameter benefit.** With 0.03 mm noise on training diameters, OOD diameter error
  fell 19.9% (0.119 → 0.093 mm; 3 of 3 seeds).
- **Sparse labels: a moderate benefit.** With diameter labelled only at months 0, 6 and 12, held-out
  error fell 34% (0.024 → 0.016 mm) and OOD error 11.6% (2 of 3 seeds).
- **Scarcity: a weaker benefit than on synthetic data.** Gains were +17.8%, +5.0% and +9.1% with 8, 16
  and 32 training designs (each 2 of 3 seeds positive). There was no gain with 4 designs (−3.2%).

**Limitations.**
- **The COMSOL device is idealised:** a solid ring with sutures 1000× stiffer than realistic. Realistic
  sutures cannot hold that ring (Section 3.3), so it stands in for the regime where sutures hold most of the
  crimp (82% at implant). Realistic sutures do reach that regime on the proposed lattice designs: the
  10 mm design is held at 13.3 mm and the taper at 14.0–15.5 mm (Section 4.2).
- **The suture-decay law is assumed,** and is shared by the simulator and the physics loss.
- **The physics loss uses the simulator's exact parameters** (geometry, suture stiffness, material
  constants). Given COMSOL's true diameter, the closure alone gives ξ to 0.005 RMSE on OOD designs, so
  ξ recovery tests the reduced-order form and the training method, not independently chosen constants.
- **Not modelled:** fatigue under cardiac cycling, plasticity, and the frame-end crowns.
- **The lattice is idealised:** flat, plane-stress, and its strut dimensions were estimated from a render.
- **Seeds:** secondary conditions use 3 seeds, so individual percentages are suggestive.
